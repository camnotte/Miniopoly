# Standard mode: Overtime ending proposal

Status: discussion proposal. This release does not change scoring, rent, GO, Festival payouts, neighborhood shifts or bankruptcy rules. Overtime is not yet a selectable option.

## Why Standard matches can stall

GO injects $200 per qualifying pass, and reward cards inject more cash. Rent transfers money between players and the Community Festival. Bank fees and taxes also enter the Festival, where later payouts return them to players. Purchases and construction remove cash, but those expenses diminish as portfolios mature. Once development slows, cash can grow faster than players lose it.

## Recommended ending: optional Overtime

Use the chosen round limit as a warning/transition into a competitive finish instead of immediately selecting the richest player. Announce Overtime two full rounds in advance. Everyone keeps their properties, improvements, cash and ability to negotiate, sell and develop.

Suggested first prototype, subject to balance testing:

1. Reduce GO income to $100 for the first Overtime round and $0 thereafter.
2. Raise rent by 25% for the first Overtime round and 50% from the second onward. Keep the 90% owner / 10% Festival division of paid rent.
3. Charge each player an Overtime fee of $50 in the first round, increasing by $25 each following round. This specific fee leaves the game permanently rather than entering the Festival. Normal Festival rules remain in effect for other payments.
4. Continue until one solvent player remains. Players resolve shortfalls by selling improvements/deeds, negotiating rent or declaring bankruptcy under the existing rules.

The permanent cash drain matters: returning the Overtime fee through the Festival would prolong the same money-circulation problem. Fees would need a clear bill, ample warning and the same accessible asset-sale controls as rent. Forced collection must not silently eliminate a player or deduct a fee twice after reconnecting. Bonuses, auctions and turn order would need explicit tests before implementation.

There should be no automatic highest-net-worth winner at the transition. Overtime creates new choices: invest to threaten opponents with higher rent, preserve liquidity, sell strategically or negotiate for survival.

## Alternative worth discussing

A visible development objective could award an immediate victory—for example, completing two color sets and building an Estate on each. This would favor construction and trading, but opponents can stall it by refusing the final trade. Overtime is a stronger first choice for reliably bringing a long match to a finish.
