# Disaster Season · Tsunami (v0.3.106)

Current rent rule (v0.3.111): rent pays 90% to its owner and 10% to the Community Festival. The Festival share rounds to the nearest whole dollar; the owner receives the remainder. Other debt payments still go entirely to the Festival. Earlier version sections below describe historical changes.

These rules apply to new matches and resets started with v0.3.103. Existing in-progress saves without the directional rule keep their previous rules until restarted; no resolved damage or elimination is undone.

## Building and sirens

Choose 10, 15 or 20 building rounds. Each player starts with two different-color residential properties totaling $400 and a Building Permit, without spending starting cash. Another permit arrives at the midpoint (round 6, 9 or 11 respectively). Paid development costs twice the Standard price and requires the full unmortgaged set; permits remain free and bypass the set for that development. Passing GO still pays $200 plus a random available unowned home, when one remains. Utilities retain their ordinary rent rules. Railroads can now be developed independently into a Depot, Terminal and Grand Station.

The first rounds are ordinary building rounds. Sirens begin at the start of the penultimate building round: round 9 of 10, 14 of 15, or 19 of 20. A direction is chosen once and saved for the entire disaster. Every player gets the final two complete building rounds to prepare; rent continues. Selling undeveloped, unmortgaged deeds to the bank still returns half their original price. Paid construction resale retains its ordinary discounted refund.

- North or south: only deeds on the top and bottom rows are exposed.
- East or west: only deeds on the left and right columns are exposed.
- Special/corner squares cannot be targeted.
- EXPOSED markings identify the eligible path, not guaranteed targets.

## Viewing the approaching wave

Open My Properties / Property Overview → **In Path of Wave** to see standing properties along the exposed edges across all three neighborhoods, with ownership and protection. Unowned properties are identified as not currently eligible; submerged sites are excluded. The path appears after the sirens reveal direction and clears when the sea is calm. It does not reveal the exact randomly selected targets.

## Five directional waves

After the final building round, wave 1 selects up to **three different owned, unsubmerged properties total across all three neighborhoods**, within the exposed edges. Selection happens at impact; no player knows the exact targets beforehand. If fewer than three are eligible, only those available are struck. Sheltered edges are unaffected. All five waves approach from the same announced direction. A surviving/rebuilt exposed property can be selected again; no cooldown is guaranteed.

Waves 2–5 follow after one further complete round each. Auctions pause normal turns and do not use up a preparation round.

| Ten-round setup | Event |
|---|---|
| Rounds 1–8 | Normal building |
| Start of round 9 | Sirens and direction revealed |
| Rounds 9–10 | Two complete preparation rounds |
| After round 10 | Wave 1; auction; round 11 starts |
| After rounds 11, 12 and 13 | Waves 2, 3 and 4, each followed by auction and normal turns |
| After round 14 | Wave 5; final auction; calm recovery round 15 |
| After round 15 | Final scoring |

## Built-in protection and Wave Shield cards

A developed residential property has one built-in flood shield. Its first unprotected hit removes one development stage and permanently spends that shield. Trading, selling or rebuilding does not reset it; additional buildings do not add extra shield uses. Later hits without a Wave Shield submerge it. Improved railroads gain the same one-use development shield; utilities cannot be developed.

Community Chest, Chance, Lucky Draw and Traffic Surprise each include a Tsunami-only **Wave Shield** card. Wave Shields have their own inventory, separate from the ordinary saved-perk limit. Hold at most two; drawing another at the limit or after calm seas pays $50 instead.

Open **Storm preparations** in Property Status. On your turn, before rolling or after resolving your landing, spend one card on one exposed, functioning property you own. Maximum one card per player per upcoming wave. The protection stays with that property if traded. It expires after the next wave even if the property is not selected. If struck, it prevents all wave damage and preserves the property's built-in shield. The board displays a blue/gold **1 WAVE** badge.

## Submerged sites and blind auctions

An unprotected hit removes the deed and all buildings from the former owner. The space becomes a named puddle with a dotted outline. It has no rent or property value; landing is safe. It cannot be bought through an ordinary landing or GO reward.

After each wave, a blind auction offers up to three unowned submerged sites, prioritizing those just destroyed. Unbid bank-held sites can appear in later wave auctions. All active players, including former owners and players with no functioning properties, submit one sealed set of bids or pass. Enter $0 to pass on a site. Combined bids cannot exceed current cash. Bids cannot be edited after submission, and other players see only who has submitted until bidding closes.

Bidding closes when everyone submits or after 90 seconds. Missing bids become passes; no host approval is required. Auctions and submitted bids survive a server restart, with the original deadline. Normal turns, movement, trades and development continue while bids are open. Sealed bids reserve cash from optional spending; required bills take priority and can convert that player’s bids to passes. Only the highest bidder pays their bid; losers pay nothing. Tied highest positive bids select one winner randomly, with the result saved once. After closing, everyone can review all bids in Storm preparations → Last auction results. No positive bids leaves the site with the bank.

## Rebuilding

Winning buys a damaged site, not a functioning deed. Its rebuilding cost is shown before bidding: half the original deed price, rounded up, with a $50 minimum. The site remains worth $0 and collects no rent until rebuilt.

On your next turn, open Storm preparations and pay to rebuild before rolling or after resolving the landing. No color set is needed for rebuilding. Base rent and original deed value return; buildings start at zero and further development follows the normal set/permit rules. A rebuilt exposed property can be struck again. Previously spent built-in flood protection stays spent.

## Calm seas and scoring

After wave 5, the ocean calms. Turns continue while outstanding auctions finish. The next full round after the final auction closes is the recovery round, so every player can rebuild before scoring. If wave five creates no auction and none is pending, that current calm round is the recovery round. No further waves occur. Highest final net worth wins: cash plus original value of functioning unmortgaged deeds plus the existing funded-development resale value. Submerged sites contribute zero. Equal net worth shares victory. These matches remain outside ranked Standard records.

Losing the last functioning property no longer eliminates a player: bidding, rebuilding, purchases, trades and GO can provide a comeback. Ordinary bankruptcy still eliminates a player, and a sole nonbankrupt survivor wins early. There are no insurance purchases or other disaster types in this update.

## Railroad improvements (v0.3.106)

Every owned, unmortgaged railroad can develop independently, without owning other railroads. Stages are Depot, Terminal and Grand Station, capped at three improvements. Each stage costs $100 normally or $200 in Tsunami. Building Permits may pay for a free stage. Rent is the ordinary $25/$50/$100/$200 ownership-count rate multiplied by 1.5, 2 or 3 (rounded up), according to the landed railroad’s stage. Only functioning, unmortgaged railroads count. Each funded improvement contributes $50 to net worth and can be sold for $50; the Tsunami surcharge does not increase resale or valuation. A wave removes one improvement and spends the railroad’s built-in shield. Further improvements cannot renew a spent shield. A Wave Shield can still protect it.

Property Status shows your personal development/protection totals, followed by All neighborhoods totals across every player and neighborhood. One property counts once even if it has both built-in protection and a Wave Shield.

## Winner receipts and personal guidance (v0.3.106)

After bidding closes, every active player sees who acquired each site and for how much. Winners receive personal rebuilding costs and next steps, including whether to wait for their turn. Got it dismisses a receipt; Show rebuilding options opens Storm preparations. Closing a receipt marks it read only in that browser/seat. Resolved receipts are retained for the five waves and unread results can be shown after reconnect. Bids remain hidden until closing.

The bottom-left helper is on by default. Read and dismiss tips so they leave the board clear, or choose Mute helper. Game Options → Player helper hints restores it. Muting optional tips does not hide auction outcomes. The eight-second wave and demolition are visual only; the host still selects targets and resolves damage once. Ruined sites show rubble and a small puddle, have no rent or deed value, and regain their original artwork and base value only after rebuilding.


## Development warnings (v0.3.107)

Develop property and Building Permit choices mark exposed owned properties with IN WAVE PATH and their separate protection status. Other exposed properties remain listed with the reason they cannot currently be developed. A spent shield is not restored by further building. Before the sirens reveal a direction, or after calm seas, no approaching-wave indicator appears. The path includes eligible edges across all neighborhoods, but only up to three owned properties total are chosen at impact. The earlier translucent wave artwork is restored without changing damage, bidding or scoring.


## Finding the player helper (v0.3.108)

Every active player can choose Game Menu → Preferences → Show player helper to turn on their personal helper and reopen the current hint. Each browser remembers its own preference and read tips. The helper includes building and waiting advice even when no owned property is exposed. Dismiss or mute it after reading. Dialogs, movement and wave effects temporarily hide tips; auction winner receipts remain independent.


## Live bidding and the board timer (v0.3.109)

The countdown above the board stays visible when the auction window closes. Click it to bid or review sealed bids; an auction shortcut is also available in landing popups. Closing before submission preserves draft amounts in that browser. Bidding does not reset or pause the normal turn. Known required payments reduce the bidding budget, and new bills can release sealed reservations by converting those bids to passes. Other players never receive your bid amounts or reservation totals before closing.

A later wave can queue another distinct set of sites while current bidding continues. Its 90-second deadline begins only when that queued auction opens. Bids, queue and current deadline survive restart. All outstanding auctions finish before the full calm recovery round and final scoring.


## Community Festival and valuation (v0.3.110)

All cash debt payments, including rent, bank fees, taxes, card penalties, Setback escape, ember damage and remaining bankruptcy cash, now enter the Festival fund. A Festival visit pays the visitor 80%, 70% or 60% with two, three or four remaining eligible players; the rest share the remaining 20%, 30% or 40%. Owners keep their deeds and improvement value but receive no direct rent cash. Past payments and completed Festival payouts are unchanged.

Improvements contribute half the standard development cost per retained valued stage to net worth, alongside cash and functioning deed value. Tsunami construction surcharges add no value; submerged sites are worth zero until rebuilt.


## Rent income and Festival funding (v0.3.111)

Rent is split 90% to the owner and 10% to the Community Festival fund. The fund share rounds to the nearest whole dollar; the owner receives the remainder, conserving the original rent amount. Partial rent collected in bankruptcy uses this same split. Bank debts, taxes, card penalties, Setback escape and ember damage still enter the fund in full. The visitor’s 80/70/60% payout with two/three/four remaining players is unchanged, as is improvement valuation. Prior paid transactions remain unchanged.
